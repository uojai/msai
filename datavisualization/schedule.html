Week 8

March 13, 2026

Hands-On Data Visualization with WHO Malaria Mortality Data

This interactive Google Colab notebook guides students through a complete data visualization workflow using real-world malaria mortality data from the World Health Organization. Starting with fundamental data loading techniques—including direct upload and Google Drive integration—students learn to inspect, clean, and filter datasets using pandas. The notebook then progresses through a series of increasingly sophisticated visualizations built with Matplotlib and Seaborn, from basic bar charts and histograms to advanced statistical plots including box plots, violin plots, and error bars. Each step includes clear explanations of both the code and the statistical concepts, helping students understand not just how to create visualizations, but when and why to use each type. By the end, students can filter data by region, country, and time period; compare trends across continents; and communicate meaningful insights about global health disparities—all while building practical Python skills applicable to any data science project.

Week 7

March 6, 2026

Beyond the Basics: Advanced Techniques & Applications in Data Visualization

This lecture moves beyond simple chart creation to explore the advanced principles and practices of professional data visualization. Participants will discover how interactivity transforms passive viewers into active explorers, learning the fundamental "grammar of interaction" that powers modern dashboards and narrative scrollytelling. Through real-world examples, we contrast visualization approaches across domains—comparing the action-oriented efficiency of business intelligence dashboards with the precision and uncertainty visualization required in scientific communication. The lecture concludes with a critical examination of ethical considerations, revealing how common design choices can unintentionally mislead and providing a practical framework for creating visualizations that are not just beautiful, but truthful, fair, and responsible. Ideal for students and professionals who understand basic chart types and want to elevate their work to the next level.

Week 6

Data Storytelling & Dashboard Design

This lecture explores the transition from raw data collection to impactful decision-making through the dual lenses of Data Storytelling and Dashboard Design. Students will learn to apply the Narrative Arc to agricultural datasets, transforming static metrics into compelling stories of conflict and resolution—such as identifying the "Silent Thief" of sub-clinical infection. Guided by Tamara Munzner’s Nested Model, we will analyze how to select effective visual idioms and channels that respect human cognitive limits and the "5-second rule" of dashboard usability. Finally, the session addresses the critical role of Audience and Context, teaching students how to tailor information density for diverse stakeholders while maintaining the ethical integrity of the visual scale to ensure every chart serves as a reliable call to action.

Chart Types and Design

In this session, students will transition from simply "making graphs" to mastering the art of visual storytelling through the 4-S Rule (Simple, Small, Scalable, and Storytelling). We will dive deep into the specific use cases for core chart types—including bar charts for comparison, line charts for trends, and heatmaps for density—learning not just how to build them in Matplotlib, but when each serves as the most effective lens for your data. Week 2

Jan 23, 2026

The Anatomy of a Chart

This lecture, "The Anatomy of a Chart," serves as the foundational bridge between the psychological "why" of human vision and the technical "how" of data visualization design. We move beyond the automatic grouping rules of Gestalt psychology to explore the "Grammar of Graphics," a systematic framework for mapping abstract data onto physical marks and visual channels. By deconstructing charts into their core building blocks—such as position, color, and size—students will learn to navigate the hierarchy of perceptual accuracy to ensure their designs are both truthful and effective. Through a practical case study involving a community livestock dashboard, we demonstrate how to select the most potent visual channels to transform raw numbers into intuitive, actionable insights for any audience.

Introduction and Logistics

This lecture provides a comprehensive introduction to the art and science of data visualization, beginning with an exploration of why translating raw numbers into visual formats is essential for efficient decision-making and storytelling. The session concludes with a practical deep dive into core design principles, such as establishing a clear visual hierarchy, utilizing high-contrast elements to direct attention, and applying strategic color theory to ensure clarity and accessibility in every graphic.
