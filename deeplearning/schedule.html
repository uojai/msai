Week 9

March 23, 2026

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)

This lecture explores the transition from theoretical neural networks to high-performance, real-world applications by focusing on Deep Learning Best Practices and Generative Architectures. Students will learn to move beyond simple accuracy to evaluate models using robust metrics like Precision, Recall, and the F1-Score, while mastering the art of Transfer Learning to solve complex problems with limited data. The session also introduces the innovative world of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), breaking down the competitive "minimax" game between a Generator and a Discriminator. By the end of this lecture, students will be equipped to diagnose common training hurdles like overfitting and mode collapse, providing them with the professional toolkit necessary to kick off their own deep learning group projects.

Deep Learning Best Practices

Building a high-performing Deep Learning model is as much an art as it is a science. In this lecture, we move beyond basic architectures to explore the 'Engineer’s Toolkit': the essential best practices for refining AI. We will master regularization techniques like Dropout and Early Stopping to defeat overfitting, and learn to interpret the Confusion Matrix alongside Precision, Recall, and F1-scores to evaluate success beyond simple accuracy. Finally, we will implement Transfer Learning using MobileNetV2, demonstrating how to leverage world-class, pre-trained 'brains' to solve custom image classification problems with high precision and minimal data.

Deep Learning Applications

In this session, we transition from the foundational theory of neural networks to their transformative applications in the real world. We will explore the "eyes" of AI through Computer Vision, covering how machines move from simple image classification to the pixel-perfect precision of instance segmentation and pose estimation. Turning to the "ears and voice" of AI, we will demystify Natural Language Processing (NLP), examining how Large Language Models (LLMs) translate languages, analyze sentiment, and generate human-like text. Finally, we bridge these concepts with Time Series Forecasting, where students will build and visualize a recurrent model to predict future trends from historical data. By the end of this lecture, you will understand the specific architectures that power today’s most advanced AI systems and gain hands-on experience implementing them in Keras.

Week 6

Deep Learning with Frameworks

This lecture provides a hands-on transition from deep learning theory to professional implementation using the industry's two leading frameworks: TensorFlow and PyTorch. We will demystify the "black box" of model training by comparing the streamlined, automated workflows of Keras with the explicit, granular control offered by PyTorch’s imperative design. You will learn how to build robust data pipelines using ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) principles and discover how Data Augmentation can artificially expand small datasets to improve model generalization. By the end of the session, you will be "framework bilingual," capable of implementing a linear regression model in both environments and understanding the critical role of automatic differentiation—the "hidden magic" that powers modern AI.

Week 5

February 16, 2026

Recurrent Neural Networks – Modeling Time and Text

This lecture explores the power of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and their ability to process sequential data where order and context are paramount. We begin by analyzing why standard feed-forward networks struggle with temporal dependencies before introducing the "hidden state"—the mechanism that gives machines a form of short-term memory. We then address the critical challenge of the Vanishing Gradient problem and examine the sophisticated architectures designed to solve it: Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU). Students will learn how "gates" control the flow of information to maintain long-term dependencies, enabling applications ranging from sentiment analysis to complex time-series forecasting.

Week 4

Convolutional Neural Networks – The Eyes of AI

This lecture dives into the "eyes" of artificial intelligence by exploring Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), the specialized architectures that allow machines to interpret the visual world. We shift from general-purpose networks to models specifically designed to handle the high-dimensional complexity and spatial relationships of raw pixel matrices. Students will trace the hierarchical journey of an image—from biological inspiration to the mathematical application of convolutional filters and pooling—learning how these layers automatically extract features like edges, textures, and complex structural shapes. By the end of this session, you will understand the structural mechanics of feature learning and how to build a CNN from scratch to recognize and classify images with high precision.

Topics: Convolution, Max Pooling, CNN Architecture, Hyperparameters

Week 3

January 26, 2026

Training Neural Networks

This lecture provides a comprehensive foundation in the mechanics of neural network training, moving beyond architecture to explore how machines actually learn from data. Students will trace the iterative cycle of learning, beginning with Forward-propagation to generate predictions and the use of Mean Squared Error (MSE) to quantify performance. We delve into the calculus of Back-propagation and the Chain Rule to understand how errors are attributed to specific weights, followed by the application of Gradient Descent to minimize those errors. The session also addresses critical practical challenges in deep learning, including the selection of Learning Rates, the advantages of Mini-batch and Adaptive optimizers like Adam, and essential regularization techniques such as Dropout and Early Stopping to prevent overfitting and ensure models generalize effectively to real-world data.

Week 2

January 19, 2026

Artificial Neural Networks

This lecture offers a compelling exploration of the bridge between biological intelligence and artificial neural architectures, providing a foundational understanding of how Deep Learning models are built and optimized. Students will trace the evolution of neural networks starting from the biological blueprint of the human brain—examining how dendrites, synapses, and axons translate into mathematical inputs, weights, and activation functions. The session moves from the fundamental Perceptron to the complexity of Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) and Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), illustrating how stacking layers and applying non-linear functions like ReLU and Sigmoid allow machines to solve complex problems. By the end of the lecture, students will understand the structural mechanics of "deep" layers and how loss functions like Binary Cross-Entropy and Mean Squared Error are used to measure and refine a model’s predictive power.

Introduction and Logistics

This introductory lecture provides a foundational overview of Deep Learning, exploring its transformative impact across fields like computer vision, autonomous systems, and natural language processing. We begin by tracing the architecture of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) back to their simplest computational unit: the Perceptron, a basic linear classifier designed to mimic biological neuron functions. The session then details the transition to Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs), illustrating how stacking layers of neurons and incorporating non-linear activation functions allow these networks to learn complex patterns within high-dimensional data.
